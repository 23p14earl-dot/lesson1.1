¡Hola! Mucho gusto.

Learn to greet someone, introduce yourself, and choose an appropriate way to speak to classmates and instructors.

Your route: Listen, Notice, Practice, Create. Everything you need is on this page. Complete each part in order.

By the end of this lesson, you can…

  1. greet classmates and instructors appropriately.
  2. ask and answer someone's name.
  3. ask how someone is and respond politely.
  4. use common greetings and farewells with accurate spelling and punctuation.

2. Notice: Essential expressions

Look closely first

Before you read any explanation, look at the expressions and questions below. What do you notice about the punctuation marks and the accent marks?

Check answers and explanation

Spanish questions begin with ¿ and end with ?, and exclamations begin with ¡ and end with !. Accent marks, as in días and tú, are part of the spelling and can change meaning: tú means "you," while tu means "your."

Greetings

  • ¡Hola! — Hello!
  • Buenos días — Good morning
  • Buenas tardes — Good afternoon
  • Buenas noches — Good evening / Good night

Introductions

  • Me llamo… — My name is…
  • ¿Cómo te llamas? — What's your name?
  • Mucho gusto — Nice to meet you
  • Igualmente — Likewise

Asking and saying how you are

  • ¿Cómo estás? — How are you? (informal)
  • Muy bien — Very well
  • Bien, gracias — Well, thanks
  • Más o menos — So-so
  • ¿Y tú? — And you? (informal)

Farewells

  • Adiós — Goodbye
  • Hasta luego — See you later
  • Hasta mañana — See you tomorrow
  • Nos vemos — See you

Speaking to classmates and professors: tú or usted?

Spanish has different ways to say you. In this lesson, use informal language with a fellow student and formal language when speaking respectfully to a professor you do not know.

Informal: tú

A classmate

  • ¿Cómo te llamas? (What's your name?)
  • ¿Cómo estás? (How are you?)
Formal: usted

A professor you do not know

  • ¿Cómo se llama usted? (What's your name?)
  • ¿Cómo está usted? (How are you?)

Notice the clues: te llamas and estás signal informal speech. se llama and está go with formal usted in these questions.

Note: This is a beginner-friendly classroom guideline. Actual use of tú and usted varies by region and relationship, and some regions also use vos or vosotros. You do not need to conjugate these verbs yet. Just recognize the two patterns.

Culture note

Greeting customs vary by region and relationship. Some people shake hands, some greet with a kiss on the cheek, and some simply wave. When you are unsure, watch what the other person does and follow their lead.

Practice B: Choose the appropriate expression

For each situation, choose the more appropriate of the two options.

  1. You are greeting a classmate at 9:00 a.m.

    1. Buenos días
    2. Buenas noches

  2. You are greeting your professor at 8:00 p.m.

    1. Buenos días, profesora
    2. Buenas noches, profesora

  3. You are leaving and know you will see your friend tomorrow.

    1. Hasta mañana
    2. Buenos días

  4. You are asking a classmate's name, using tú.

    1. ¿Cómo te llamas?
    2. ¿Cómo se llama usted?

  5. You are telling someone your name.

    1. ¿Cómo te llamas?
    2. Me llamo…

  6. Someone says Mucho gusto when meeting you.

    1. Igualmente
    2. Adiós

1A, 2B, 3A, 4A, 5B, 6A

4. Create: Your turn

Create three short exchanges. These are open-ended. The examples are models, not the only correct answers.

  1. Meet a new classmate. Say hello, introduce yourself, and ask their name.
  2. Politely greet a professor you do not yet know.
  3. Say goodbye to a classmate whom you will see tomorrow.

Choose the format that works best for you: write your answers, record audio or video (for example, in Canvas Studio), or sign them. If another format would work better, ask your instructor.

Check that your exchange fits the situation (informal or formal) and, if you wrote it, that you included opening question marks and accents.
